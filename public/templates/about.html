THE LESSON

THE ACCOUNT I LOST TAUGHT ME THE MOST.

I used to do SEO the conventional way. Find search volume, pick keywords, check competitors, build pages, chase rankings, report traffic.

Then I worked with a self-managed super fund (SMSF) specialist. That approach got them more traffic and better rankings, but not enough new clients.

So I went back to their onboarding data. Their strongest source of business wasn't search. It was referrals from accountants. The question changed from where do people search for SMSF auditors? to where do the referral partners cluster?

I scraped accounting-firm density across Melbourne suburbs and built location pages around the densest areas. Traffic more than tripled in about two months.

The account ended before the strategy could mature. As a commercial engagement it failed. As a lesson, it changed everything I've done since.

3×+organic traffic in about two months, from pages built on referral-partner density
Endedbefore the strategy matured. I count it as a loss and say so.
WHAT I TOOK FROM IT

Search demand isn't always the best proxy for commercial opportunity. Sometimes whatever creates the demand is worth more than the demand itself.

SIGNALS OTHER
MARKETERS MISS.

Since then I look for signals that show where customers are, how concentrated they are and how ready they are to buy, even when keyword tools show almost nothing.

GROUP BOOKINGSFire safety training client

FIRE WARDEN TRAINING

Fire warden training sells to workplaces, mostly as group bookings. Instead of treating it as a consumer search product, I mapped where the training is required and where the client was willing to travel. Then I built regional location pages with substantial supporting content.

In the best month, the client took four group bookings at about $1,200 each, plus one booking of about 150 students rolling out over the rest of the year.

$25K+attributable revenue in that month, against a $1,000 monthly SEO engagement
DENSITY DATAFirst aid training

CHILDCARE FIRST AID

Keyword tools showed tiny volumes. So I used a national registry of childcare centres as the dataset and ranked suburbs and cities by how many centres they hold.

Each centre is a potential group-training customer. Search volume in those areas can be close to zero while commercial intent stays high.

The same model as fire warden training, applied to a different registry.

HOW I WORK

HOW I CHALLENGE A BRIEF.

I'll push back when something fundamental looks wrong. I don't assume I'm right, so I work through it in this order:

  1. Ask why the recommendation exists.
  2. Name the hypothesis behind it.
  3. Ask what evidence supports it.
  4. Test my own assumptions against that evidence.
  5. Identify the likely problems.
  6. Offer an alternative, if the evidence supports one.

The aim is to find the thinking behind a decision and test it.

SEE MY NINE-STEP STRATEGY METHOD →

Art and science.

The art is people, positioning, creativity and communication. The science is measurement, experiments, data and causal reasoning.

I'm sceptical of marketing advice that stays at the level of principle, with no mechanism you can test.

MY RULE

Test it first. Understand why it worked. Then teach the principle.
