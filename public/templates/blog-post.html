SEARCH DEMAND IS NOT ALWAYS THE BEST PROXY FOR OPPORTUNITY.

Keyword volume tells you how many people type a phrase. It doesn't tell you where the buyers are. For local services, operating density often predicts demand better than any keyword tool.

THE SHORT VERSION
  1. Search volume measures phrasing, not buyers. Low-volume suburbs can hold the highest real demand.
  2. Score locations on what drives demand. For childcare first aid, that's centres, educators and young families.
  3. Roll out in tiers. Full pages where demand is real, hub pages where it isn't.

Most local SEO rollouts start with a keyword export. Sort by volume, write pages for the top suburbs, and work down the list. It feels rigorous because it has numbers in it. It's also why so much programmatic local content fails quietly: the numbers measure the wrong thing.

Where volume misleads

Keyword tools model how often a phrase is searched. At suburb level, most of those estimates are rounded to zero or bucketed so coarsely that the difference between two suburbs is noise. The ranking you get from them is a ranking of the tool's sampling, not of the market.1

The density model

Childcare first aid demand clusters where the people who need the certificate concentrate. So instead of asking how many people search for the course in a suburb, ask what generates the need there.

  • Childcare centres operating in or near the suburb
  • Educator headcount those centres employ
  • Young families who book the parent version of the course

Each suburb gets a density score from those inputs. The score becomes a hypothesis you can test against bookings, which a keyword volume figure never was.

Tiering the rollout

Scoring every suburb doesn't mean writing a page for every suburb. The score decides how much content each location earns.

Full pages where demand is real

High-scoring suburbs get a dedicated page with local proof: nearby centres served, session times, venue details. That's information only a page about this suburb can carry.

Hub pages where it isn't

Lower-scoring suburbs roll up into a regional hub. That avoids a set of near-identical pages that add nothing and dilute the site.

What transfers

The tactic here is childcare-specific. The principle isn't. Before you rank locations by search volume, name what actually creates demand for the service, find a data source for it, and score on that.

  1. Find the mechanism that generates demand.
  2. Score locations on it, not on phrasing.
  3. Test the score against real bookings before scaling.
